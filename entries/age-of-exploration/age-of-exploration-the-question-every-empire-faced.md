<!-- section: GLOSSARY OF SUBJECT VOCABULARY -->

<!-- block 4 role=content -->
**empire** (EM-pyre) — a group of many lands and peoples ruled by one government or ruler.

<!-- block 5 role=content -->
**trade route** (trayd root) — a path that traders travel again and again to carry goods between distant places.

<!-- section: A blocked road to Asia -->

<!-- block 20 role=content -->
By the late 1400s, European merchants had wanted goods from Asia for a long time: silk, spices, and gold traveled west along overland **trade routes** that passed through the Ottoman **empire**. When the Ottomans took control of the eastern Mediterranean, those routes became harder and more expensive to use. European traders needed another way to reach Asia, and Portugal had already started looking for one by sailing south and east around Africa.

<!-- block 21 role=content -->
Spain wanted a route of its own. In 1492, an Italian sailor named Christopher Columbus convinced the Spanish crown to fund a different plan: instead of sailing around Africa, he would sail west, straight across the ocean, to reach Asia from the other side.

<!-- section: What Columbus believed he found -->

<!-- block 24 role=content -->
In October 1492, Columbus's ships reached land. Columbus was certain he had done exactly what he set out to do: reach the edge of Asia. He called the people he met "Indians," believing he was somewhere near the Indies. He was wrong about where he was — but the mistake mattered less, at first, than what he reported home: land, and a route to it.

<!-- section: HELPFUL TO KNOW -->

<!-- block 26 role=content -->
Columbus never admitted he had reached a place unknown to Europe. Even after several voyages, he insisted he was near Asia. That single wrong assumption is part of why the word "Indies," and later "Indians," stuck to a hemisphere it never actually described.

<!-- section: The problem no plan accounted for -->

<!-- block 28 role=content voice -->
Here is what none of the European planning had accounted for: the Americas were not empty, and they were not Asia. They were home to millions of people, organized into societies as different from one another as Spain was from France. Every European power that showed up after Columbus had to answer a question nobody in Lisbon or Madrid had needed to ask before: **what do you do about the people who are already here?**
