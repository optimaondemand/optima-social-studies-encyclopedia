<!-- section: GLOSSARY OF SUBJECT VOCABULARY -->

<!-- block 4 role=content -->
**Querecho** (keh-REH-cho) — the name Coronado's expedition used for Apache bands living on the southern plains before they had horses.

<!-- section: Cíbola and Fray Marcos's report -->

<!-- block 17 role=content -->
Fray Marcos de Niza traveled north ahead of Coronado and returned with a report describing what he believed might be Cíbola, one of seven fabled golden cities. His actual claims were cautious and uncertain. By the time the story had passed through enough retellings to reach an army ready to march, that uncertainty had disappeared. What began as a maybe had hardened into a fact everyone acted on.

<!-- section: PART 2 THE LLANO AND QUIVIRA -->

<!-- block 19 role=content -->
Coronado's march of 1540–42 crossed the Llano Estacado, a vast, flat expanse with no landmarks, no wood, and no reliable water. An army needs all three to function: orientation to know where it is going, fuel for cooking and warmth, and water to survive. The Llano offered none of them freely, and the march nearly came apart because of it.

<!-- block 20 role=content -->
On these plains, Coronado's men encountered the **Querecho**, Apache bands living here before the horse arrived, moving on foot in a landscape that offered no easy travel for anyone.

<!-- section: WATCH OUT -->

<!-- block 22 role=content voice -->
Do not picture the Querecho the way you would picture the mounted Comanche of later centuries. The horse has not reached the southern plains yet. This is a different society, moving on foot, under different constraints entirely.

<!-- section: Quivira at last -->

<!-- block 24 role=content -->
After all of that, Coronado finally reached Quivira, likely in present-day Kansas. He found grass houses, not gold. The central hope of the entire expedition, a second Tenochtitlán, was not fulfilled.
