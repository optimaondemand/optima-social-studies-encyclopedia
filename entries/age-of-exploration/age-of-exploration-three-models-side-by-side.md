<!-- section: GLOSSARY OF SUBJECT VOCABULARY -->

<!-- block 3 role=content -->
**empire** (EM-pyre) — a group of many lands and peoples ruled by one government or ruler.

<!-- block 4 role=content -->
**trade route** (trayd root) — a path that traders travel again and again to carry goods between distant places.

<!-- block 5 role=content -->
**colony** (KAH-luh-nee) — a settlement of people living in a new territory but still ruled by their home country.

<!-- section: Three empires. Three answers. One question. -->

<!-- block 8 role=content -->
Spain, France, and England all faced the same problem: a populated land they wanted to claim. Each power answered it differently, and each answer fit a different kind of place. Here are all three models, before history tests which one actually took hold in Texas.

<!-- section: Why the model matters more than the map -->

<!-- block 26 role=content -->
A model built to conquer an existing empire will not work in a place with no empire to conquer. A model built around a handful of traders will not populate a continent. Keep this in mind: the question is not only which power reaches Texas first, but which model actually fits what Texas turns out to be.

<!-- section: THE CENTRAL QUESTION -->

<!-- block 32 role=content -->
Which of these three models — and which power — ends up controlling Texas, and why that one? Keep this question in view.
