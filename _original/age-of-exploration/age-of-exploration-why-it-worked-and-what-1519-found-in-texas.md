<!-- section: GLOSSARY OF SUBJECT VOCABULARY -->

<!-- block 3 role=content -->
**conquistador** (kon-KEES-tuh-dor) — a Spanish soldier who took part in conquering land in the Americas for Spain.

<!-- block 4 role=content -->
**encomienda** (en-koh-mee-EN-dah) — a Spanish system that forced conquered Native people to work for Spanish colonists, usually without pay.

<!-- section: PART 1 WHAT SPAIN ACTUALLY CAPTURED -->

<!-- block 16 role=content -->
When Tenochtitlán fell, Spain did not inherit an empty city to fill however it liked. It inherited a tribute and labor system that already existed, with millions of people already organized to produce goods and pay tribute to whoever sat at the top. Spain simply took over the top position.

<!-- block 17 role=content -->
This system had a name: **encomienda**. Under encomienda, Spanish colonists were granted the right to demand labor and tribute from a specific group of Native people. The land mattered, but the real prize was the population itself: an already-organized workforce Spain did not have to build from scratch.

<!-- section: PART 2 THE SAME YEAR, A DIFFERENT COAST -->

<!-- block 19 role=content -->
In that same year, 1519, a Spanish expedition led by Alonso Álvarez de Piñeda sailed along and mapped the Texas Gulf coast. Where Cortés found a capital of 200,000 people and an empire's worth of tribute waiting to be inherited, Piñeda found a coastline with no cities at all.

<!-- section: WATCH OUT -->

<!-- block 21 role=content -->
It is tempting to think Spain simply had not gotten around to Texas yet in 1519. That is not quite it. Piñeda's map already told Spain something specific: whatever this place was, it was not going to work the same way Mexico had.

<!-- section: The central hypothesis -->

<!-- block 23 role=content -->
Here is the central question: what happens when a conquest model built around inheriting a tribute system reaches a place with no tribute system to inherit? Spain is about to spend three hundred years finding out.

<!-- section: THE CENTRAL QUESTION -->

<!-- block 25 role=content -->
Mexico gave Spain a system to inherit. Texas will not. Keep watching for what Spain tries instead.
